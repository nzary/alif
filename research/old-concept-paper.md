## What it argues

Individuals can only become AI literate if their organizations give them the conditions to do so. The article describes **organizational learning debt**: the gap that builds up when an organization keeps deploying AI faster than it builds the competencies, learning infrastructure, culture and governance that the AI requires. ALiF's components and levels serve as the reference against which that gap is measured.

## Four dimensions

- **Capability:** knowledge and skills below the levels the Standard specifies.
- **Infrastructure:** weak feedback loops, evaluation routines and communities of practice.
- **Cultural:** norms that value throughput over learning.
- **Governance and pipeline:** gaps in AI governance, and erosion of the experience through which future experts develop, a concern sharpened by AI taking over junior-level work.

The dimensions interact. A shortfall in capability produces poor feedback, which creates infrastructure debt, which sustains a culture that undervalues learning. Fixing one dimension alone gives diminishing returns. The full theory is summarized on the [Organizational Learning Debt]({{ '/theory/organizational-learning-debt/' | relative_url }}) page.

## Status

The article has been submitted to PLOS ONE and is under peer review. ALiF documents do not cite it as published until it is. If peer review changes the dimensional structure, the related materials will be updated.

## Why it matters for the ecosystem

Two organization-level instruments depend on it: the [OLD Maturity Assessment]({{ '/instruments/old-maturity-assessment/' | relative_url }}) and the [OLD Infrastructure and Culture Audit]({{ '/instruments/old-infrastructure-culture-audit/' | relative_url }}). Their final design is tied to the outcome of review. The Standard Roadmap also asks whether the four dimensions hold as distinct constructs and whether learning debt predicts outcomes such as safety incidents and speed of adaptation.
