## What it is

The AI Launchpad Course is the entry point to the ALiF program ecosystem. It takes two to three hours, is fully online and self-paced, and can be completed in one sitting or over a few sessions within a week. It needs no prior knowledge of AI and no technical background.

Some ALiF documents call it the **AI Awareness Course**, its earlier name. The current syllabus (v2.0) uses the name AI Launchpad.

It is an orientation, not a competency program. People who complete it have not shown AI literacy; they have started working toward it. The course introduces all five ALiF components (TEC, DAT, EVA, ETH, PRA) at a level below L1 Foundation and claims no sub-competency.

## Structure

| Module | Focus | Components | Time |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| 1. Understanding AI | What AI is and is not, how it learns, five ways it fails (including automation bias) | TEC | 45 to 50 minutes |
| 2. AI in your world | AI in the learner's own work, and the difference between AI that supports judgment and AI that replaces it | TEC, PRA | 40 to 45 minutes |
| 3. Why AI literacy matters | Individual reasons and the shared responsibility for patient safety, research integrity and fair assessment | ETH, PRA | 30 to 35 minutes |
| 4. Your ALiF journey | The five components, why data literacy comes before evaluation and ethics, the three levels, and the next step | All five | 30 to 35 minutes |

From Module 2 onward, learners choose a role track (Clinician, Researcher, Educator, Administrator or Student, with a General track for others). The objectives and sequence are the same for everyone; the examples and scenarios change.

## Assessment and completion

Assessment is formative and completion-based. Each module ends with a short knowledge check with feedback and no pass mark. Learners also complete a classification activity, a written reflection that stays in their personal record unless they choose to share it, and a short self-reflection on confidence across the five components. That self-reflection is labeled as confidence only, not evidence of competency.

Completion earns a **Certificate of Orientation**. It is not a competency certificate and does not give an ALiF level. Institutions that record completion in HR or professional development systems should label it as AI literacy orientation.

## Where it leads

The course points each learner to the [20 to 25 hour foundation program]({{ '/programs/ai-launchpad/' | relative_url }}), where competency development at L1 begins. The [Implementation Pathway Model]({{ '/companions/implementation-pathway-model/' | relative_url }}) recommends the orientation before any baseline self-assessment, so that participants share a basic vocabulary.

An Arabic version is in development as a full adaptation rather than a translation, following the [Arabic Localization Protocol]({{ '/companions/arabic-localisation-protocol/' | relative_url }}).

## Versioning and alignment

The course follows the ALiF Program Versioning and Review Policy: annual content checks, an alignment review after each Standard release, and a major review after a Standard major version. It will state its alignment with ALiF 2.0 while keeping its own version number. See the [Versioning and Review Policy]({{ '/governance/versioning-policy/' | relative_url }}) for how the Standard itself changes.
