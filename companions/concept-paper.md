## What it is

The Concept Paper is where ALiF was first described as a model: an account of what AI literacy means for professionals, organized into components and progression levels, with principles for adapting it to different roles. It is a theoretical document, not a set of requirements.

## How it relates to the Standard

When the ALiF Standard was first released as v1.0, it consolidated its structure from three sources: Concept Paper v2, the [Competency Framework]({{ '/companions/competency-framework/' | relative_url }}) v2 and supporting documents. The Standard is now the canonical, normative reference. Where the Concept Paper and the Standard differ, the Standard applies.

## Status

The ecosystem inventory in the [Architecture Document]({{ '/governance/architecture-document/' | relative_url }}) lists the Concept Paper as a Core document at version 2, published as a preprint and last reviewed in February 2026. Publication details are not reproduced here.

## Who should read it

The Architecture Document recommends that researchers start with the Concept Paper and the [Development Protocol]({{ '/companions/development-protocol/' | relative_url }}): the first explains the theoretical model, the second the validation method. Policy makers and program leads will usually find the [Standard]({{ '/standard/' | relative_url }}) and its companion guides more useful.

## What comes next

Companion documents are being reissued as version 2.0 alongside ALiF 2.0. The structure of ALiF (five components, sixteen sub-competencies, three levels) is unchanged, so the core model described in the Concept Paper still holds. Further details of the reissued paper will be published when available.
