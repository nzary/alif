<p>Ethical Considerations is one of the five components of AI literacy in the ALiF Standard. Every conforming implementation addresses all five, with emphasis that varies by professional role.</p>
<h2>Key elements</h2><ul><li>Ethical principles and frameworks</li><li>Policy awareness and compliance</li><li>Risk management</li><li>Governance approaches</li></ul>
<h2>Example competencies</h2><ul><li>Understands and applies ethical guidelines in AI use</li><li>Adheres to institutional policies on AI</li><li>Identifies and mitigates potential risks</li><li>Contributes to ethical AI governance</li></ul>
<h2>How it relates to the other components</h2><p>Ethical Considerations (ETH) as cross-cutting. Ethical reasoning is not confined to the ETH component. Every component has ethical dimensions: how data is collected (DAT), how tools are used (TEC), how outputs are assessed (EVA), and how implementations are designed (PRA). The dedicated ETH component provides the explicit frameworks and governance structures, but ethical awareness permeates the entire framework.</p>
<h2>Sub-competencies (3)</h2>
<section class="subcomp" id="eth-1">
<h3>ETH.1 Responsibility</h3><p>The ability to use AI in ways that are transparent, accountable, and consistent with professional and ethical standards. This sub-competency encompasses understanding the ethical principles governing AI use, properly attributing AI contributions to work products, communicating openly about AI use to colleagues and stakeholders, and making ethical judgments about when and how to deploy AI tools.</p>
<p class="change"><strong>Proposed in ALiF 2.0:</strong> Adds disclosing AI-generated or AI-altered media in professional communication, so that others can tell synthetic content from records of real events.</p>

<section class="subcomp" id="eth-2">
<h3>ETH.2 Compliance</h3><p>The ability to operate within the institutional policies, regulatory frameworks, and professional guidelines that govern AI use. This sub-competency encompasses knowledge of relevant policies at organizational, professional, and governmental levels; the capacity to document AI use in ways that satisfy compliance requirements; and the ability to help establish or improve governance structures where they are lacking.</p>
<div class="table-wrap"><table><caption class="sr-only">Level descriptors for ETH.2</caption><thead><tr><th scope="col">Foundation (L1)</th><th scope="col">Intermediate (L2)</th><th scope="col">Advanced (L3)</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Knows relevant institutional policies on AI use. Follows established documentation requirements. Adheres to professional guidelines. Identifies when policy guidance is unclear or missing.</td><td>Implements compliance procedures for AI use in team settings. Creates documentation systems satisfying governance requirements. Assists in developing or refining AI policies. Monitors compliance across defined workflows.</td><td>Develops compliance frameworks for organizations that balance accountability with innovation. Leads policy development initiatives that incorporate stakeholder input. Designs sustainable governance systems that adapt to evolving regulatory landscapes.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>
</section>
<section class="subcomp" id="eth-3">
<h3>ETH.3 Risk Management</h3><p>The ability to identify, assess, and mitigate risks associated with AI use in professional contexts. This sub-competency encompasses recognizing potential harms (privacy violations, bias amplification, misinformation, dependency risks), evaluating the likelihood and severity of identified risks, and implementing safeguards and mitigation strategies.</p>
<p class="change"><strong>Proposed in ALiF 2.0:</strong> Adds synthetic media and AI-mediated influence, and dependency risks to expertise: skills that erode, fail to develop or develop incorrectly through reliance on AI, and lost practice opportunities for others.</p>
